Signing in

How a person, an agent or a machine proves who it is. Loom checks identity in one place, at the door.

  • Signs a person in with a passkey or a one-time link.
  • Gives each agent's body and each machine a token of its own.
  • Lists your signed-in browsers and passkeys, and lets you end them.
  • Sends notifications to the phones and browsers you turn on.

How it works

Callers, sessions, passkeys and what happens next

Three kinds of caller

  • a persona browser session
  • an agent's bodya token of its own
  • a machinea token of its own

Every caller from outside loom presents a token. A token belongs to one member, one agent or one machine. This is the one place loom checks identity.

Sessions

SessionChrome on Macsigned in with a passkey · stands for jonathan's token

A person's browser holds a session, which stands for one of their tokens. An agent driving a browser gets a session that lasts six hours.

Passkeys and links

  • passkeyregistered once, used each time
  • sign-in linkworks once, then expires

A person gets a session with a passkey or by opening a one-time sign-in link. A passkey's counter goes up with each use, so a lower number than last time means a copied key.

After that, labels

tokenknown callerlabels

Once loom knows the caller, the labels on each record decide what it may see. Loom's own parts never present a token to each other.

Who can see this

  • aYour own. A person sees their own passkeys, signed-in browsers and notification devices, and can end each one.
  • bOwners. Household owners see the household's tokens and sessions and can revoke them.
  • cNever the token. Loom stores a one-way fingerprint of each token, session cookie and sign-in link.
