Measuring

Loom keeps count of how long things take, so slow spots are visible. It does this on your own machine, and only when a household owner turns it on.

  • Off unless a household owner turns it on. Off costs nothing.
  • Instrumented once, in the standard way monitoring tools understand.
  • Sends measurements to a tool you already run, to loom's own store, or both.
  • Lets agents read the local store, so work that judges loom has real numbers.

How it works

The switch, the two destinations, the store and its readers

Off by default

Off

Measuring is off unless a household owner turns it on. Off costs nothing, and someone who never sets it up never sees it.

One instrumentation, two destinations

  • outside toolany that accepts the standard format
  • local storekept by loom itself

Loom is instrumented once with OpenTelemetry, the standard monitoring tools understand. Turned on, the same measurements go to a tool the household already runs, to loom's own store, or both.

What the local store holds

  1. requesttasks · 412 times · typical 12 ms
  2. operationcreate a task · 9 times · typical 6 ms
  3. jobreading GitHub · 288 times · slowest 132 ms

A rolled-up record per day of how long each kind of request, operation and background job took, and how often it ran.

Why agents read it

Real numbersDid the change make things faster?three weeks before · three weeks after

Work that judges loom itself has real numbers to go on: comparing two harnesses, or checking whether an experimental change made things faster or slower over a few weeks.

Who can see this

  • aWho sees it. Household owners and agents.
  • bWho turns it on. A household owner. Until then nothing is counted and nothing is shown.
